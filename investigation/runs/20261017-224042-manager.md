## Question

> Which observed problems deserve attention first, and what should happen next?

## Answer

**Bottom line:** The biggest problem on the cloud platform is its image clean-up job. Almost all of its warning messages come from that job, it never stops, and it crashed 8 times. A few slow VM starts and one faulty supercomputer node come next.

## What we looked at
Warning and error messages from the cloud platform that creates virtual machines (VMs) for users, covering about 26 hours between 14 and 17 May 2017. We also looked at the Blue Gene/L supercomputer from June 2005 to January 2006.

## Top priorities
1. **The image clean-up job keeps reporting files it doesn't recognise, and sometimes crashes**
   **Measured:** About 3,100 of 3,300 warning messages (9 in 10), about 130 an hour, in all 26 hours that have data. The job also crashed 8 times.
   **What it probably means:** Leftover disk files are building up, or the job is set up wrong. This noise could hide real problems.
   **Owner:** Storage & Images, helped by Platform Runtime.
   **Next step:** Ask them whether these files are safe and why the job crashes.
   **Confidence:** High. The pattern is large and steady.

2. **A few VMs took twice as long to start**
   **Measured:** 4 VMs flagged as problem cases took about 37 seconds to start, against about 20 seconds for the other 2,000.
   **What it probably means:** Something specific to those VMs slowed them down. It is not a platform-wide trend.
   **Owner:** Compute / Virtualization.
   **Next step:** Ask for a short explanation of what went wrong with those 4.
   **Confidence:** Medium. Only 4 cases.

3. **One supercomputer node flooded the logs**
   **Measured:** About 152,000 memory-error messages came from a single node in one week of June 2005. That is about 15 in 100 of all the supercomputer's warning and error messages.
   **What it probably means:** One faulty machine, not a problem across the whole system.
   **Owner:** HPC Node Software.
   **Next step:** Confirm the node was repaired or replaced. Stop counting it as a system-wide problem.
   **Confidence:** High.

## What we can't tell from the logs
- Whether users noticed any of this.
- Why the clean-up job crashes.
- Whether the faulty node was ever fixed.
