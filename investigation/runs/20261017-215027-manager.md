## Question

> Which observed problems deserve attention first, and what should happen next?

## Answer

**Bottom line:** On the cloud platform, one image clean-up job causes 9 in 10 warning messages and also fails now and then, so fix that first. On the supercomputer, the biggest error count came from a single machine in a single week, so it is not a system-wide problem.

## What we looked at
The cloud platform that creates virtual machines (VMs) for users, over 14–17 May 2017, and warning and error messages from the Blue Gene/L supercomputer, June 2005 to January 2006.

## Top priorities
1. **Image clean-up job keeps flagging files it doesn't recognise**
   **Measured:** about 3,000 of 3,300 warning messages (9 in 10), about 130 an hour, in all 26 hours that have data. The job also stopped with an error 8 times.
   **What it probably means:** leftover image files are not being cleaned up, and the job itself is unreliable.
   **Owner:** Storage & Images, with Platform Runtime for the job failures.
   **Next step:** ask Storage & Images to explain the unknown files and fix the clean-up.
   **Confidence:** High. The pattern is large and steady.

2. **Some VMs start slowly**
   **Measured:** the 4 VMs already flagged as faulty took about 38 seconds to start. The other 2,060 took about 20 seconds, so the flagged ones were about 2 times slower.
   **What it probably means:** the faulty VMs had a problem during start-up.
   **Owner:** Compute.
   **Next step:** ask Compute why those 4 were slow.
   **Confidence:** Low. There are only 4 cases.

3. **One supercomputer machine flooded the logs with memory errors**
   **Measured:** about 152,000 messages came from 1 machine in 1 week of June 2005. That is about 15% of all supercomputer warning and error messages.
   **What it probably means:** one faulty machine, not a wider failure.
   **Owner:** HPC Node Software.
   **Next step:** confirm that machine was repaired or retired. Don't read the raw totals as system-wide trouble.
   **Confidence:** Medium. The source is clear, but the cause is not.

## What we can't tell from the logs
- Whether users noticed any of this, or whether it cost anything.
- Why the clean-up job finds unknown files.
- The cloud logs cover only about 2 days and have gaps, so we can't see longer trends.
