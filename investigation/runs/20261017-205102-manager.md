## Question

> Which observed problems deserve attention first, and what should happen next?

## Answer

**Bottom line:** No problem in these logs is clearly hurting users. The most useful thing to do now is ask two supercomputer teams to explain two short floods of error messages, each from a single machine. The cloud platform's biggest source of warnings looks like harmless noise and can wait.

## What we looked at
Logs from the cloud platform that creates virtual machines (VMs) for users, covering 14–17 May 2017 with some gaps, and from the Blue Gene/L supercomputer, covering June 2005 to January 2006.

## Top priorities
1. **One supercomputer machine flooded the logs with memory errors**
   **Measured:** about 152,000 error messages in one week of June 2005, all from one machine. That is 15% of all the supercomputer's warning and error messages.
   **What it probably means:** a single faulty machine, not a problem across the whole system.
   **Owner:** HPC Node Software. **Next step:** ask whether that machine was repaired or taken out of service.
   **Confidence:** Medium. The source is clear, but the logs don't show the cause.

2. **A supercomputer control computer kept hitting an internal safety check**
   **Measured:** about 84,000 messages from one control computer, almost all on two days (3 August and 20 September 2005).
   **What it probably means:** a software fault that came back in bursts.
   **Owner:** HPC Control System. **Next step:** ask whether a fix was made after September.
   **Confidence:** Medium. There were only 4 bursts and the cause is unknown.

3. **The cloud's image clean-up job keeps reporting files it doesn't recognise**
   **Measured:** about 3,100 of 3,300 cloud warning messages (9 in 10), about 130 an hour, in all 26 hours we have data for.
   **What it probably means:** routine noise that hides real warnings.
   **Owner:** Storage & Images. **Next step:** ask the team to confirm it is harmless and stop it being logged as a warning.
   **Confidence:** High. The pattern is steady and very regular.

**Stop worrying about:** slow VM starts. Normal starts take about 20 seconds every time (out of about 2,060). Only the 4 VMs already known to be problems were about 2 times slower.

## What we can't tell from the logs
- Whether any of this affected users or work running on the systems.
- What caused the two supercomputer bursts.
- What happened during the gaps when no cloud data was collected.
